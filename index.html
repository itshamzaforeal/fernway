The approach

Three things we believe, that most coaching doesn't say out loud

Evidence

Clarity comes from testing, not thinking.

You cannot reason your way into a career you've never tried. Most people spend years journaling toward a decision that one real Tuesday of actually doing the thing would settle in an afternoon.

Sequence

"What's next" is the wrong first question.

Before you can answer that, you need to know what's actually wrong with what's now — the work, the people, the pace, or something you haven't named yet. Skip that step and you'll reinvent your way into the same problem with a new title.

Tolerance

Competent people get stuck longer.

Being good at something buys you the ability to tolerate it. That tolerance is the trap — it lets you go five more years past the point where someone less capable would already be gone.

How it works

Four stages, run in order

The line stays neutral while you're still figuring out what's wrong. It turns the moment you start testing — that's the actual pivot, not the day you quit.

01

Name what's actually wrong

Not "I'm unhappy." The specific mechanism: the work itself, the pace, the people, or a ceiling you can already see from here.

02

Map real options

Against your actual constraints — the mortgage, the school year, the market you're actually qualified for. Not a fantasy list of careers.

03

Test before you leap

Small, cheap, reversible experiments that produce real evidence. A weekend project outperforms a year of "someday."

04

Make the move

On a timeline, with a plan for the risk. Deliberate, not dramatic — a decision, not a scene.
